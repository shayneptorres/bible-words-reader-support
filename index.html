What it is

Bible Words Reader is an iPhone app for reading the Greek New Testament and the Hebrew and Aramaic Old Testament. Tap any word to see its dictionary form, a short English gloss and its full parsing.

  • An optional word-for-word English line under each verse.
  • Vocabulary lists built from any passage, or from presets such as Greek words used more than 100 times, studied with spaced-repetition flashcards.
  • Reading plans with real calendar dates, a daily goal, a reading streak and an optional daily reminder.
  • Notes and bookmarks.
  • On devices with Apple Intelligence, describe a reading plan or vocabulary list out loud and let the on-device assistant build it.

iPhone, iOS 27 or later. Works offline — the whole Bible and its word data come with the app. No account, no ads, no tracking.

Common questions

Which Greek and Hebrew texts does the app use?

The Greek New Testament comes from the Open Greek New Testament (OpenGNT), based on the Nestle 1904 text, with glosses from STEP Bible. The Hebrew and Aramaic Old Testament, with its word-by-word parsing, comes from the Open Scriptures Hebrew Bible. Full credits and licences are under Text sources.

Does it work offline?

Yes. The biblical text, dictionary forms, glosses and parsing are all bundled with the app, so reading, word lookups, flashcards, plans, notes and bookmarks all work without a connection. Only the optional links out to other apps and websites (such as YouVersion, BibleGateway or Logos) need the internet.

What happens when I tap a word?

You'll see the word's dictionary form (lemma), a short English gloss and its full parsing — part of speech plus tense, voice, mood, person, number, gender, case or stem as they apply.

What is the word-for-word ("wooden") line, and how accurate is it?

It's a rough English rendering of each word, kept in the original word order, so you can follow the Greek or Hebrew without switching to a translation. It's built from dictionary glosses and the verb's parsing, not written by a translator, so it reads awkwardly on purpose and is sometimes simply wrong. Narrative and letters read best; Hebrew poetry and the prophets are noticeably rougher. Treat it as a window into the source text, not a translation.

How do I turn on the word-for-word line?

It's off by default. In the reader, tap the Tools button (the grid of four squares), choose Reader settings, and switch on Show wooden translation. It appears in the Verse by verse layout, which you can pick in the same screen.

How do the flashcards decide when I see a word again?

After each card you rate how well you knew it. The schedule is a variant of the classic SM-2 method:

  • Good — first review in 1 day, then 6 days, then the gap grows by the word's ease (up to 2.5×) each time.
  • Easy — a new word jumps straight to 4 days, and known words grow faster than with Good.
  • Hard — still counts as remembered, but the gap only grows a little and the word gets slightly harder to space out.
  • Again — the word comes back tomorrow and its gaps grow more slowly from then on.
What kinds of vocabulary lists can I make?

Build a list from any passage — a chapter, a book or a range — or start from a preset: Greek or Hebrew words that occur more than 100, 75, 50, 25, 10 or 5 times. On devices with Apple Intelligence you can also describe the list you want out loud.

How does the reading streak work?

Any day you mark reading counts toward your streak. You get one "freeze" a month, which covers a single missed day so one busy day doesn't erase a long run. Your longest streak is kept even after a streak ends.

Why didn't my daily reminder arrive?
  • Notifications are off. If you declined the permission, the reminder settings say so and offer a shortcut to iOS Settings to turn notifications back on for the app.
  • You already read that day. The reminder is skipped on purpose on days that already have reading.
  • You haven't opened the app for a while. The reminder is scheduled one day at a time and refreshed whenever you open the app or record reading, so after a stretch away it resumes once you open the app again.
  • A Focus mode or Scheduled Summary may be holding it back — check iOS Settings → Notifications.
What does the voice assistant need?

An iPhone that supports Apple Intelligence, with Apple Intelligence turned on and its on-device model finished downloading. It also asks for microphone and speech-recognition permission the first time you use it. Speech is transcribed on your device and requests are handled by Apple's on-device model — nothing is sent to me or to any server. If your device can't run it, the voice options are hidden or explained and you can build lists and plans by hand as usual.

Does it sync between devices or use iCloud?

No. There's no account and no iCloud sync — your lists, study progress, plans, notes and bookmarks live only on the iPhone where you created them.

How do I back up my study data?

The app has no export or sync of its own. Your data is included in your iPhone's regular backup (iCloud Backup, or a backup to a Mac or PC), so restoring your iPhone — or setting up a new one — from a backup brings it back.

How do I reset or delete my data?

Swipe to delete individual vocabulary lists, reading plans, notes or bookmarks. To erase everything at once, delete the app from your iPhone — that removes all of its data. (Restoring an older iPhone backup can bring that data back.)

How do I see the introduction again?

Tap Tools (the grid of four squares) → Settings, then under Help choose Show Onboarding. The reading plans and vocabulary tours are there too. Replaying a tour doesn't change your reading position, plans or goal.

What about accessibility?

You can change the reading text size under Reader settings, and the app follows your system light or dark appearance. If something is hard to use with VoiceOver, larger text or any other accessibility setting, please email me — I'd like to fix it.

Is there an iPad version?

Not yet — Bible Words Reader is designed for iPhone for now.

Bible Words Reader collects nothing. Your vocabulary lists, study progress, reading plans, notes and bookmarks stay on your iPhone. There are no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no third-party services. Voice input is processed on your device and is not sent to me or stored anywhere.
